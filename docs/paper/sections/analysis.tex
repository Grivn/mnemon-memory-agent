\subsection{\jev{} Against LLM Judges}
\label{sec:system1}

\sys{} judges records with a decision model rather than an LLM.
To test this choice, we asked gpt-4.1-mini and DeepSeek the question \jev{} answers, with the same dialogue and records, for 599 LoCoMo and \lme{} questions (14,359 records, 1,004 of them gold evidence).
\jev{} separates gold evidence best (\cref{fig:system1}): AUC 0.942 against 0.900 for DeepSeek and 0.853 for gpt-4.1-mini, and 0.939 against 0.872 and 0.798 on \lme, whose chat-turn records rarely repeat the question's words.
All three put almost every gold record in their top 16; \jev{} draws its yes line conservatively (precision 0.76, recall 0.60) and the LLMs generously (precision 0.42--0.45, recall 0.68--0.79).
A \jev{} call on 24 records takes 0.34\,s and costs \$0.00044 while answering two questions per record; DeepSeek takes 1.05\,s and \$0.00066, and gpt-4.1-mini 3.74\,s and \$0.0017, for one.
With three to five such calls per question, several on the critical path, an LLM judge would add seconds and cost while separating evidence less well.

\subsection{What Consolidation Adds}
\label{sec:consolidation}

Answering the same questions with the index removed measures what consolidation adds to \sys.
With gpt-4.1-mini answering it adds \consLoCoMoAbs{} points on LoCoMo, \consLMEAbs{} on \lme, \consBeamAbs{} on BEAM-100K and \consBeamTenAbs{} on BEAM-10M, and with the reasoning reader \dsConsLoCoMoAbs{} on LoCoMo and \dsConsLMEAbs{} on \lme, each with a 95\% interval above zero; on HaluMem the two judges disagree in sign.
The gains fall where a question does not point to its evidence: instruction following, knowledge updates, summarization and abstention on BEAM; knowledge updates and multi-session questions on \lme; and temporal questions, for which the timelines supply dates.
Consolidation multiplies the cost per question by \consCostRange, mostly for \jev{} to judge the index items.
